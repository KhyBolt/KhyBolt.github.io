Data and reproducibility

The replay used two public research recordings and one UTC day of historical tracks. The replay method, archive query and original overlay metadata record the inputs, score bins and solver revisions. The archive query checks input counts and digests. Reproducing the ranking and the 321 m estimate requires pinned solver revisions that are unavailable through the current public links. This map keeps the same bins and changes their colour and opacity.

The study reference overlay uses the observer coordinates published in the study’s results table and points from the matching KML files. The solver’s fitted point and scored track geometries are unavailable in the public replay bundle.

How the search worked.
  1. Find two sustained falling tones in the recording, before looking at the flight tracks.
  2. Use candidate flight tracks and sound travel time to predict pitch at each receiver position, allowing one shared offset between the audio and track clocks.
  3. Refine the best-fitting areas. Return a point only when the search passes the benchmark’s checks.

The checks are not a guarantee. A deliberately constructed pair of tracks with indistinguishable Doppler curves still produced a wrong result. The 14.2 m error above applies only to this fixture.

All audio and tracks are simulated. This page replays a saved run; it doesn’t process other recordings or look up aircraft.
